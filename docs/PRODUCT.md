# Product brief

## Problem

Many people reach for short-form feeds immediately after waking because the interaction cost is nearly zero and the next reward is unpredictable. Most learning tools ask for a much larger commitment: choose a course, resume a lesson, watch a long video, or open an editor.

ByteScroll reduces that activation energy without making learning passive.

## Target user

The first user is a beginner who wants to learn Python or system design but struggles to begin consistent practice. They have a phone, five to ten minutes, and little appetite for a traditional lesson first thing in the morning.

## Core promise

> Replace the next few passive scrolls with one useful technical idea—and keep going only when you choose.

## MVP loop

1. The learner opens ByteScroll.
2. A session matching the learner's 5, 10, or 20-card goal is already prepared.
3. Each unit teaches, demonstrates, and then asks for active recall.
4. The learner receives immediate, concise feedback.
5. The system updates XP, mastery, and spaced review.
6. At the checkpoint, the learner can finish, review mistakes, or continue for five more cards.

## Product principles

### Active by default

No card should be satisfied by scrolling past an explanation. The learner must make a small commitment first.

### Earn attention; do not trap it

ByteScroll should be engaging, but success means the learner can leave after completing a useful session. Dark patterns, punitive streaks, and endless feeds are outside the product thesis.

### Measure retention

Minutes, taps, and session length are diagnostic metrics. The primary outcome is whether the learner answers a related question correctly after time has passed.

### Keep teaching content reviewable

Generated drafts must pass automated checks and human review before entering the canonical curriculum.

## MVP success metrics

| Metric | What it reveals | Initial target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Checkpoint completion | Is the starting session appropriately sized? | 60%+ |
| Day-7 return | Does the habit persist past novelty? | 25%+ |
| Delayed recall | Are learners retaining concepts? | 70%+ |
| Wrong-to-right conversion | Does review correct misconceptions? | 50%+ |
| Median session time | Is the experience truly lightweight? | 5–8 minutes |

Targets are hypotheses to validate, not claims about current performance.

## Explicit non-goals

- Live AI generation of unreviewed lessons
- Social follower counts or competitive leaderboards
- A general-purpose code execution environment
